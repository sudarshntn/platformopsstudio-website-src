After two decades in enterprise platform engineering, I kept running into the same pattern: organizations sink real money into GitOps tooling — Kubernetes clusters, Argo CD, automated pipelines — and the investment quietly underperforms anyway. The pipelines get built. The operators get configured. And teams drift back to manual workarounds within a few months.

The tools were never the bottleneck. The people running them were never given the time or structure to actually get ready. That gap is what pushed me to write my first eBook, **Empowering Teams: Training and Onboarding in the GitOps Framework**.

## What the book covers

It takes a reader from GitOps fundamentals through enterprise-scale rollout, across topics including release management and continuous delivery, Kubernetes deployment patterns with Argo CD and Flux, automated rollback design, compliance and audit trails for regulated industries, observability with Prometheus and Grafana, microservices and service-mesh considerations, CI/CD tool integration, large-enterprise governance and RBAC, disaster recovery, and — the chapter I care about most — building real onboarding and mentorship programs so GitOps knowledge outlives any one engineer.

## The pattern behind failed adoptions

When a GitOps rollout stalls, it's almost always one of three things: teams don't share a common mental model of how the system actually works, nobody owns the new-hire onboarding experience, or there's no feedback loop connecting a deployment outcome back to what the team learns from it. All three are fixable — they just require the same kind of intentional investment teams already give to their code: documentation, training time, mentorship, and retros that actually change the process next time.
